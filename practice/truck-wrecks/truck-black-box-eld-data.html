Black Box & ELD Data.

The truck records what the driver will not admit: speed, braking, and hours.

Home / Practice Areas / Truck & 18-Wheeler Wrecks / Black Box & ELD Data

The driver will say the light was green and the speed was safe. The truck keeps its own record, and it does not take sides.

Modern trucks are rolling data recorders. The engine control module logs speed, throttle, and hard braking in the moments around a crash. The electronic logging device records the driver's hours. Together with dashcam footage, telematics, and the maintenance and driver qualification files, this data can reconstruct exactly what happened and whether the rules were followed.

The catch is that much of it is controlled by the trucking company and much of it is overwritten or discarded on a schedule. Once a truck is repaired or returned to service, key data can be gone. That is why a formal preservation letter, sent in the first days after a crash, is often the single most important step a lawyer takes. It puts the company on notice that destroying the evidence carries its own consequences.

Common Questions
What is a truck's black box?
It is the engine control module, which records data such as speed, throttle, and braking around the time of a crash. Combined with the electronic logging device that tracks the driver's hours, it can reconstruct the wreck far more reliably than any witness.
Can the trucking company erase the data?
Much of it can be lost in the ordinary course once a truck is repaired or put back in service, and some can be overwritten quickly. A preservation letter sent early puts the company on legal notice to keep it, which is why acting fast matters so much.
