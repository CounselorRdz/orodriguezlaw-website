Fatigue & Hours of Service.

A tired trucker is a dangerous one.

Home / Practice Areas / Truck & 18-Wheeler Wrecks / Driver Fatigue & Hours of Service

A tired driver behind eighty thousand pounds is a loaded weapon, and federal law has said so for decades by limiting how long that driver can stay on the road.

Fatigue is one of the most common and most preventable causes of serious truck wrecks. Federal hours-of-service rules exist precisely to stop it. A property-carrying driver may drive no more than eleven hours within a fourteen-hour on-duty window, only after ten consecutive hours off, must take a thirty-minute break after eight cumulative hours of driving, and is capped at sixty hours in seven days or seventy in eight. These are not suggestions. They are the law of the road for commercial trucks.

The hours-of-service limits appear at 49 C.F.R. Part 395. Since the electronic logging device mandate, those hours are recorded automatically, which makes a violation far harder to hide than in the paper-log era.

Why fatigue happens anyway

The pressure runs downhill. Drivers are often paid by the mile and pushed by dispatch to hit delivery windows that the clock does not allow. Some falsify status, some drive through the limits, and some are simply scheduled into exhaustion. When a crash follows, the question is not only whether the driver was tired but whether the company's pay and dispatch practices made fatigue the predictable result.

The proof is in the data. Electronic logs, dispatch records, fuel and toll receipts, and the truck's own engine data can be lined up against the timeline of the crash to show how long the driver had really been going. That reconstruction is often what turns a denied claim into an admitted one.

Common Questions
How do you prove a truck driver was fatigued?
By the records, not the driver's word. Electronic logging data, dispatch and delivery schedules, fuel and toll receipts, and the truck's engine data can be assembled to show the real hours behind the wheel and any violation of federal limits.
What are the federal limits on a trucker's hours?
For property-carrying drivers: no more than eleven hours driving within a fourteen-hour window after ten hours off, a thirty-minute break after eight cumulative driving hours, and a weekly cap of sixty hours in seven days or seventy in eight, under 49 C.F.R. Part 395.
