Eagle Pass sits where a national commercial artery meets an international bridge. U.S. 57 runs east from the border to Interstate 35, U.S. 277 runs north to Del Rio, and both carry tractor-trailer traffic that turned into commercial cargo at the crossing that morning. When a family here is hit by a truck on one of those roads, the case that follows is not a Houston case, not a San Antonio case, and not a case that belongs at a call center. It is a Maverick County case.

I am Orlando Rodriguez. I am a Houston trial lawyer, and I take serious truck, car, and industrial injury cases from Maverick County and Eagle Pass. The office is in Houston; the depositions, the hearings, and the trial happen where the case is. What follows is what I want families in this county to know before they call anyone, and why the shape of a Maverick County truck case is different from a case on Interstate 10 or Interstate 45.

The corridor

U.S. 57 is the road that most defines the Maverick County truck case. TxDOT has already studied it for interstate designation because the freight volume on it, from the international bridge east through Batesville and La Pryor and on to Interstate 35, exceeds what the two-lane corridor was designed to carry. That mix, tractor-trailers among passenger cars on a road that in stretches has no shoulder, produces the head-on and the crossing-truck crash that is the Maverick County signature.

U.S. 277 runs north out of Eagle Pass toward Del Rio, another two-lane commercial corridor with the same physics and the same pattern. Loop 480 circles Eagle Pass and carries the traffic that comes off the bridges before it disperses into the state highways. And the farm-to-market roads between them: FM 1021, FM 1908, and the roads that feed the ranches and the city, where a stop sign at a two-lane intersection meets a fully loaded truck at seven in the morning.

The commercial pattern that Eagle Pass adds to the ordinary South Texas mix is the border. Every truck that comes across the international bridges enters a highway system it does not have to enter anywhere else in the county, and it enters carrying cargo whose paperwork was signed a few hundred yards south. That is a piece of every Maverick County truck case worth mentioning to a lawyer whose first move is to preserve the carrier’s file.

The court

Under the Texas venue rule, a suit for injury or wrongful death from a Maverick County crash is usually filed in Maverick County, in front of a jury from Maverick County (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.002). The state district court that serves Maverick County sits in Eagle Pass. That matters for a serious case because a hometown jury, from the same county the crash happened in, is the mechanism the venue rule protects. It is not the courtroom in Houston, and it is not the courtroom in San Antonio. It is the courtroom on the courthouse square.

When a Maverick County case belongs in federal court, it goes to the Western District of Texas, Del Rio Division, which holds court in Del Rio. Federal cases from this corridor most often arise where a large trucking company is diverse from the family, or where a federal question, such as a civil rights claim, is in the case.

Why a truck case here is not a bigger car case

A tractor-trailer at highway speed carries its own record: the engine control module, the electronic logging device, dash camera video if the fleet uses one, dispatch records, the driver’s hours-of-service log, and the maintenance file. Every one of those is subject to being overwritten, lost, or explained away in the first weeks after a crash. The first thing I do on a Maverick County truck case is put the carrier on written notice to preserve it, before the truck is back in service and before the ELD cycle wraps.

The trucking company liability page on this site explains why the company, not just the driver, answers for hours, training, hiring, maintenance, and the decision to run a trailer with a thirty-mile-an-hour rear guard on a highway where traffic runs seventy. The Guard and Underride pages explain the physics: a passenger car that goes under a trailer never uses the front end it was built with, and a car whose front end never engages is not a car in a crash, it is a car that ran into a wall it could not see.

Two clocks run from the day of the crash. The general limitations period for injury and wrongful death in Texas is two years (§ 16.003). When the claim is against a city, a county, or the state because of the road, the barrier, or a government vehicle, written notice must reach the governmental unit within six months (§ 101.101), and some cities require notice even sooner. Those deadlines do not move because the courthouse is a long drive from Houston.

The lawyer from out of town

Families in Eagle Pass sometimes ask whether they should hire a Houston lawyer or one down the street, and the honest answer is that it depends on the case. A small case belongs with a good local lawyer. A serious one, against a trucking company with a defense firm already assigned within hours of the crash, belongs with whoever will actually try it. The insurance company knows which lawyers settle and which ones pick juries. That reputation, not the address on the letterhead and not the award badges lawyers pay for, is what sets the value of your case.

What I bring here is the same thing I bring in Harris County. One lawyer on the file. Every deposition, hearing, and argument taken personally. A caseload kept small enough that yours is never a number. A trial date treated as the goal, not a threat. And Spanish, which on this site is not a translation layer added afterward. Every page has a Spanish twin, so a parent can read the same thing a son or daughter reads in English, and every conversation with me can happen in either language.

Common Questions
Do you take truck accident cases in Eagle Pass and Maverick County?
Yes. Serious truck, car, and industrial injury cases from Maverick County and Eagle Pass. The state district court sits in Eagle Pass, and federal cases from Maverick County go to the Western District of Texas. The office is in Houston; the depositions and the trial happen where the case belongs.
Where do most Maverick County truck cases happen?
On U.S. 57 east toward San Antonio, on U.S. 277 north toward Del Rio, on Loop 480 through Eagle Pass, and on the farm-to-market roads that feed both. The commercial crossings at the international bridges add heavy tractor-trailer traffic that other counties do not have.
Do I have to travel to Houston?
No. The first call is a phone or video call, in English or Spanish. Depositions and hearings happen where the witnesses and the court are, and I travel to them. You are not handed to a case manager.
How long do I have to file?
Generally two years from the date of the crash or death under Section 16.003 of the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code. If the claim is against a city, county, or the state, written notice is due within six months, sometimes sooner. Call before the first deadline, not after.
