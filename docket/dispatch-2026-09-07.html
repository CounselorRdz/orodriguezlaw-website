Wrong Way, Labor Day

Leer este parte en español →

Three wrong-way, head-on crashes on Texas highways in five days. Two of them overnight. All three inside the holiday weekend the state had already flagged for stepped-up patrols. This is the pattern, reported the way the newsrooms reported it, and then connected to the pages on this site that explain why it keeps happening and what the law does with it.

San Antonio, Sunday, September 6. Just before 3 a.m., a driver going the wrong way on Interstate 37 South near Southton Road collided head-on with another vehicle, according to San Antonio police. The wrong-way driver died at the scene. A 23-year-old man in the other car was alert when he was pulled out and was taken to a hospital with major injuries. Police said the investigation is ongoing and did not say what caused the driver to enter against traffic. KSAT has the report.

Duncanville, Thursday night, September 3. A vehicle heading north in the southbound lanes of U.S. 67 near Danieldale Road struck an oncoming car and clipped several others, according to Dallas County sheriff’s deputies. One person in the car that was hit died at the scene; two passengers were seriously hurt. Deputies detained the driver at the scene. By the weekend, police said the vehicle had been reported stolen in Irving, and the driver was charged with intoxication manslaughter, intoxication assault, and unauthorized use of a vehicle. A charge is an accusation, not a finding. FOX 4 reported the crash and NBC 5 reported the charges.

Houston, Wednesday, September 2. Overnight, two vehicles met head-on in a wrong-way crash on the Sam Houston Tollway westbound at Telephone Road. Both drivers died. One vehicle caught fire and its driver was trapped inside. A Harris County Precinct 2 deputy constable told reporters investigators could not yet say which vehicle had been traveling the wrong direction, and nothing was released about impairment. ABC13 has the report.

The backdrop. The Department of Public Safety ran its Labor Day enforcement period from September 4 through September 7, with troopers focused on speed, impaired driving, and the Move Over law. Its own numbers from the 2025 Labor Day period: 63,015 citations and warnings, 211 DWI arrests. The DPS release is here. Cities layered no-refusal initiatives on top, meaning a suspect who refuses a breath or blood test is met with a search warrant for blood, signed by a judge on call; Austin’s police department describes its version, which ran nightly through September 7.

What the pattern says. Wrong-way entries cluster after midnight, and the usual path is an exit ramp taken as an entrance by a driver whose eyes and judgment are no longer doing the work. The physics of what follows is on this site under Closing Speed: in a head-on the two speeds add, and the energy climbs with the square of that number, which is why a crash that looks survivable at sixty is not survivable at a combined one hundred twenty. The chemistry is under The Reaction: what alcohol does to the divided attention, the night vision, and the reaction time that might otherwise have caught the wrong ramp. None of that decides any of the three cases above. It explains why the same wreck keeps arriving on the same nights.

What the law does with it. Two new entries follow this dispatch. No Refusal explains what a no-refusal weekend actually is, why Texas now needs a warrant for a blood draw, and how that blood result later travels into a civil case. Intoxication Manslaughter explains the felony, why no intent to hurt anyone is required, and why a charge under that section changes what a civil jury is allowed to award. For the practical side, the drunk driving accidents page covers the civil case, the criminal case running beside it, and the bar that overserved.

No one in these three crashes is named here, and nothing on this page assigns fault in an open investigation. The point of a dispatch is narrower: to keep the record of what happened on Texas roads this week, from the outlets that were there, next to the pages that explain it.
