Outweighed

When a loaded truck and a car collide, the same crash happens at two different speeds. The truck barely slows. The car, and everyone in it, is stopped, spun, or shoved backward in a few feet. That difference has a name in physics. It explains who dies in truck crashes, and when the trucker is the one who does.

In a collision the two vehicles trade momentum, and momentum is mass times velocity. What one loses the other gains, so each vehicle’s change in speed is set by the other’s weight. If one vehicle weighs ten times what the other does, the lighter one takes about ten times the change in speed. That change, not the number on the speedometer, is what the bodies inside have to absorb, which is the whole point of Delta-V.

The national numbers follow the physics. In 2023, crashes involving large trucks killed 5,472 people. Seven in ten of them were in the other vehicles. Eighteen percent, 961 people, were in the trucks. It is why the tired driver in The Clock is usually the one who walks away.

The more revealing number is where those 961 died. Of them, 596, about six in ten, were killed in crashes where the truck was the only vehicle involved. The truck’s weight protects its driver from a car. It does nothing against a ditch, a rollover, or a bridge pier. Against a thing that will not move, the heavy vehicle is the light one, which is The Fixed Object with a bigger vehicle and the same physics. The Roll is the other half: a tall, loaded trailer that tips turns the cab into the thing that is crushed. And Thrown Clear is what happens when the cab does not keep its driver. Against another loaded truck, the ratio is one to one again.

When the trucker is the one who dies. The law follows the family, not the size of the vehicle. When the person who died was driving the truck, the questions are the ones asked for anyone killed on a highway, with two more. Who caused it: the car that failed to yield, the maker of a cab that did not hold, the shop that did the brakes? And who was he to the company: an employee, whose employer’s workers’ compensation coverage may shape the family’s first claim, or an owner-operator, whose status turns on who controlled the work, the question Statutory Employer takes up. Texas gives the surviving spouse, children, and parents the wrongful death claim, and the estate the claim the driver himself would have had (Wrongful Death).

Mass decides who absorbs the speed. It does not decide who was at fault. That is still the question the case is built to answer.

The national figures: NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts, Large Trucks: 2023 Data, DOT HS 813 717 (5,472 people killed in crashes involving large trucks in 2023; 70 percent occupants of other vehicles, 18 percent large-truck occupants, 12 percent nonoccupants; 961 large-truck occupants killed, 596 in single-vehicle crashes and 365 in multiple-vehicle crashes). The physics is the conservation of momentum. This is general information, not medical or legal advice.
