Forum

Leer esta página en español →

A trucking case can have three addresses: where the driver lives, where the company is based, and where the crash happened. Forum is the question of which courthouse hears the case. The answer decides more than the drive to court.

The pattern. An injured driver from Texas, a trucking company based in another state, a crash on a highway in a third. This page names no case and decides nothing about any crash.

Texas courts are open. A Texas statute says an action for death or personal injury “may be enforced in the courts of this state” even though the wrongful act took place in another state or country, if a law of that place or of Texas “gives a right to maintain an action” and the suit is filed within the time Texas law allows. A resident of another state or country must also file within the time limit of the place where the wrongful act happened, and a citizen of a foreign country can sue here only if that country “has equal treaty rights with the United States on behalf of its citizens” (Section 71.031(a)). A Texas court also needs power over the company itself, a separate question that turns on the company’s ties to Texas.

Which county. Inside Texas, the general rule sends a lawsuit to the county “in which all or a substantial part of the events or omissions giving rise to the claim occurred”; or, for a person, to the county where the defendant lived when the claim arose; or, for a company, to the county of its principal office in Texas. Only if none of those applies does the suit go to the county where the plaintiff lived when the claim arose (Section 15.002(a)). Other venue rules can change the answer. When the crash happened in another state, no defendant lives in Texas and the company has no principal office here, that last rule can point to the injured Texan’s home county.

Whose law. Since 1979 Texas has decided which state’s law governs an injury case by asking which state has the most significant relationship to it, under the Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws. The Texas Supreme Court said that tort cases “will be governed by the ‘most significant relationship’ test,” and it overruled the old rule that the law of the place of the injury always controls: “The common law doctrine of lex loci delicti in this state is hereby overruled” (Gutierrez v. Collins, Tex. 1979). The statute says a Texas court applies “the rules of substantive law that are appropriate under the facts of the case,” while Texas law governs procedure (Section 71.031(b), (c)). Which state’s law applies can change what can be recovered and how fault is divided.

Sending it away. A defendant can ask a Texas court to stay or dismiss an injury or death case because it would be more properly heard in a forum outside Texas, under factors such as whether another forum exists and gives an adequate remedy. But the court “may not stay or dismiss” on that ground the claim of a plaintiff who is a legal resident of Texas, or of a derivative claimant of one (Section 71.051(b), (e)).

The rules on the road. The federal rules on a truck driver’s hours and records follow an interstate carrier across state lines; the paper they require is the same wherever the crash happens (Logbook). Where the suit is filed changes the courtroom, not the logbook.

Forum is not paperwork. Where a case is filed decides which judge, which jury, which law, and sometimes which deadline. It is the first decision in the case, and it is made before anyone argues fault.
