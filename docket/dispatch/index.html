What Dispatch is

The Night Docket’s news strand: what happened on Texas roads and in Texas courts, reported from credible outlets and agency releases, with no names and no conclusions about open investigations, then connected to the entries that explain the science and the law. Dispatches stay on the front of the ledger for about ninety days and live here permanently after that, by month. The Spanish twin of every dispatch is in El Parte.

The Blackout

Sometimes the crash that looks like one driver’s plain mistake is a stroke, a seizure or a faint that took the driver before the car ever left its lane, and Texas law does not ask only what the driver did; it asks whether he could have seen it coming. The loss-of-consciousness defense, the one word it turns on, how it sits beside the sudden emergency, why the record and not the account decides it, and who still pays when no one was at fault.

The Hood Line

A low car strikes an adult in the legs and the body wraps up onto the hood, which gives a little. Raise the front past a child’s center of balance and the same strike throws the child forward and down in front of the wheels, onto pavement that gives nothing. The crash institute found hoods above forty inches about forty-five percent more likely to kill, speed multiplies shape, and Texas asks a driver for due care to anyone on foot and proper precaution on seeing a child.
