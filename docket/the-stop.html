The Stop

NEW STRAND: The Body · the science of what an injury does.
THE CLAIM: Mental anguish after an injury is not a mood. It is a documented chain of chemistry.
WHY IT MATTERS: The element juries are asked to take on faith has a molecular address.

A man runs forty miles a week for twenty years. Then a shoulder tears, a knee folds, or a surgeon says six weeks off. He heals on schedule. His mood does not. The body mends and the man underneath it comes apart, and the people around him call it a bad attitude.

It is not attitude. It is subtraction. Something his body made every day simply stopped being made.

The factory in the legs

Here is what almost no one hears. A working muscle is not just meat and levers. It is a gland. When it contracts, it dumps chemical messengers into the blood, and some are addressed to the brain. One line runs like this: hard movement flips on a master switch called PGC-1alpha, which turns on a protein called FNDC5, which is cut into a hormone named irisin, which crosses into the brain and tells the hippocampus to make more BDNF. BDNF is the one to remember. They call it fertilizer for neurons, and the name is too modest. It grows new connections, keeps old ones alive, and drives the birth of entirely new neurons in the one region that runs memory and mood. Every run was a delivery. The legs were writing letters to the brain.

The high was never the endorphins

You were told the runner's high is endorphins. It is not. Endorphins are too big to cross from blood into brain. The euphoria that flooded that last mile came from endocannabinoids, the body's own version of the molecule in cannabis, small enough to slip past the gate and switch off the alarm. So the man was not chasing a metaphor. He was self-dosing, on a compound he brewed himself, every day, for decades. Then the prescription stopped.

What the stop takes

Cut the supply and the brain notices fast. In adults who train regularly, as little as two weeks without it produces measurable change in the hippocampus, the mood-and-memory core starting to thin for lack of use. The chemistry and the feeling move together. Controlled studies that pull committed exercisers off their routine watch depressive symptoms and fatigue climb within days of the last workout, then lift when movement returns. Read that twice. Take the movement away and you can bring on the low mood in a lab, on schedule, in people who were fine the week before. The client who says the injury changed who he is has the literature on his side.

Why the brain treats it as danger

Now the deeper question. Why should a torn rotator cuff reach in and dim a man's mind? Because the brain was built on one assumption that holds until injury breaks it: the body moves. Human thought evolved yoked to endurance movement, to walking and running down food across miles, so tightly that scientists argue the brain came to require exercise the way it requires oxygen. To a nervous system shaped over that span, stillness is not rest. Stillness is the state of the trapped, the sick, the cornered. The old machinery reads a body that cannot move and concludes something is wrong, and it sounds an alarm the man cannot argue himself out of. That is the reptile under the runner. It keeps one score. Can you still go when you have to.

The counsel table

So when a former athlete tells a jury he stopped sleeping and stopped feeling like himself after the wreck took his legs out from under him, he is not fishing for sympathy and he is not padding a claim. He is reporting a chain with a molecular address: muscle to hormone to BDNF to mood, severed at the first link by an injury he did not choose. Mental anguish is not the soft corner of the case. It is the best-documented injury in the file. The bone shows on the X-ray. This shows in the blood.

One discipline, because credibility wins cases and nothing else does. The spine of this, movement builds BDNF and lost movement costs mood, is bedrock. The ornaments, the precise part played by rhythm, the two hemispheres trading signals, are still being mapped, so we do not oversell them. Claim only what the science will carry into a courtroom. It carries more than enough.
