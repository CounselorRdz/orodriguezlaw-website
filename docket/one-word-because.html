One WordEntry 007

Because

Every case is a story, and every story runs on one engine: because.

Jurors do not weigh evidence the way scales weigh sugar. They build a story, and a story is just events chained together by causes. The side whose becauses snap together cleanly usually wins, and the side stuck saying “and then” usually loses. “And then” is a list. “Because” is a verdict getting dressed.

Psychologists figured out decades ago that the word itself carries force: people grant requests at a higher rate when a reason is attached, even a thin one. The human ear hears “because” and relaxes, assured that order exists, that someone can explain. Juries are twelve human ears in a row.

So here is the test of a case theory, and it fits in one sentence. Write your whole case as a single because-sentence. “This wreck happened because a company chose its schedule over its safety rules.” “A man lost his rights because an officer decided the rules were optional that night.” If your because-sentence works, everything you file is just that sentence growing evidence. If it does not work, you do not have a theory yet. You have a file.

Now the discipline, because this word invites attack. Every because you assert is a door you open: the defense lives entirely in the space between your cause and your effect, wedging in weather, luck, the plaintiff, anything. So assert the because you can carry all the way, and concede the noise out loud before they do. One strong cause, honestly held, beats four maybes stacked in a trench coat.

The move: write your one-sentence because at the top of your trial notebook. Every deposition question, every exhibit, every witness either feeds that sentence or wastes the jury’s time. Read it before you draft anything. It is the sentence the foreperson will say out loud in the jury room, with your name nowhere on it. That is the goal: your because, in their mouths.

One word. It carries the whole case.

Filed after midnight.
