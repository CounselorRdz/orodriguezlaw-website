The MastersEntry 015

Barbara Jordan

SUBJECT: Barbara Jordan, 1936 to 1996. Fifth Ward, Houston. Texas Southern debate. Congress.
STUDIED: Statement to the House Judiciary Committee, July 25, 1974
SIGNATURE: Earned slowness. The claimed document. Conviction without ornament.

She came out of the Fifth Ward, and when she spoke, Congress took notes.

Barbara Jordan grew up a preacher’s daughter on the north side of Houston and learned to argue on the Texas Southern debate team, where her squad went up against Harvard and did not blink. First Black woman in the Texas Senate. Then Congress, and then the night the country met her: thirteen minutes before the House Judiciary Committee in July 1974, on whether a president should be impeached.

She opened by telling the room the truth about the document in front of her. When the Constitution was finished, she said, she was not included in its We, the people. Amendment by amendment, decision by decision, she had been added. And then the sentence that carried the night: “My faith in the Constitution is whole; it is complete; it is total.”

Move One: The Earned Slowness.

She spoke slower than anyone else in the chamber and never once filled a pause. Slowness reads as authority for exactly one reason: every word is load-bearing.

The steal: cut your speed in your next hearing, but earn it first. A pause is a claim that the next words matter. Make the claim true.

Move Two: The Claimed Document.

She did not lecture about the Constitution. She held it like the deed to her own house, a document that had been withheld from her and was hers now. The room believed her reading because she owned the text.

The steal: stop citing the statute and start owning it. The lawyer who treats the text as a personal possession is the one the room believes has actually read it.

Move Three: Conviction Without Ornament.

Whole. Complete. Total. No metaphor, no garnish. Three plain predicates climbing a staircase.

The steal: when the stakes are highest, strip the sentence to the studs. Ornament spends credibility. Plainness banks it.

A Warning from the Tape.

None of it was technique alone. Behind the cadence stood total preparation and a life that backed every word. Rhythm without substance is a costume, and a jury can smell a costume from the back row. Do the work first. The voice comes second.

The Drill.

Take the last paragraph you wrote for a jury. Delete every adjective. Read what is left out loud at half speed. Whatever survives is the argument. If nothing survives, you did not have one.

Filed after midnight.
