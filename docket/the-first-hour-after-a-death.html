The First Hour: After a Death

Leer esta página en español →

Part 3 of 3. In the first hours after a death, a family is asked questions before anyone has answered theirs. Nothing has to be decided that night. A few things only have to be kept.

Who investigates. A death in a crash or at work is looked at by more than one office: the police agency that worked the crash, and the medical examiner or justice of the peace who looks into the cause of death (Inquest). After most deaths at work, the employer must report to OSHA within eight hours of learning of it; a crash on a public road outside a construction work zone is an exception (29 CFR 1904.39; Part 2). From each office, a family can ask for a name, a report number and how to request the records later.

The autopsy. It is the record of what the body went through. When more than one impact or more than one cause is possible, it is where sorting them out begins (Two Cars, One Gone).

The clocks. The clocks that matter later start now. Most claims for a death must be filed within two years. A claim against a government needs notice much sooner. And if none of the spouse, children or parents brings the wrongful death claim within three calendar months after the death, the executor or administrator “shall bring and prosecute the action unless requested not to by all those individuals” (Section 71.004(c); Questions: deadlines after a death).

What can be kept. The vehicle and its data (No Witnesses), the phone, the clothing, the names of anyone who was there, and every card and number handed to the family that night. The other side’s insurer may call early. No law requires a family to give that insurer a recorded statement.

What the law will ask later. Whether the person was conscious, and for how long, belongs to the survival claim, and it has to be proved (Conscious). The first responders’ reports, the 911 recordings and the witnesses’ accounts are where that proof usually comes from, and each of them is easier to get early than late.

Nothing has to be decided in the first hour after a death. A few things only have to be kept.

Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 71.004, as posted on texas.public.law (verified May 26, 2025); 29 CFR 1904.39, as posted by Cornell LII.
