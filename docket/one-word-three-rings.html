Three Rings

Barnum did not invent the circus. He invented the problem of where to look.

For centuries, a circus meant one ring. One act at a time. The audience’s eye had one place to go, and the performer earned it. Then in 1881, Barnum added a second ring. A few years later, a third. Three acts, running simultaneously, each demanding attention the moment the last one paused. The audience’s neck cranked left, then right, then center, then left again, and by the end of the show, they could not tell you exactly what they had seen. Only that there had been so much of it.

That is not a bug. That was the invention.

The three-ring format was a business decision disguised as a spectacle. More acts meant more tickets, more towns per season, more revenue per tent. But the deeper trick was psychological. A spectator watching one ring can judge the act. A spectator watching three cannot. Divided attention does not evaluate. It absorbs.

Every defense lawyer worth their fee has read Barnum, whether they know it or not.

Watch a complicated case for long enough and you will see the three-ring move. Ring One: a motion that raises a technical objection to your expert. Ring Two: a discovery dispute that generates two hundred pages of briefing about nothing that matters. Ring Three: a public relations posture designed to keep the client’s board of directors calm. Meanwhile the actual case, the one about what their driver did on a Tuesday morning, sits quietly in the center of the tent, waiting to be tried. The defense hopes the jury’s neck is too tired to look.

The countermove is older than Barnum, and it is Sun Tzu’s. Choose the ground. Refuse the three rings. Force the fight into one issue, one witness, one question, and stand in the doorway of it until the trial ends. When opposing counsel starts a sideshow, do not chase it. Chasing is what the sideshow was built to accomplish.

The lawyer who lets the case become a three-ring circus has already lost the doorway.

The move: audit your last motion. If it is trying to accomplish four things, it is a sideshow. Pick the one that decides the case. Everything else is a ring you built to distract yourself.
