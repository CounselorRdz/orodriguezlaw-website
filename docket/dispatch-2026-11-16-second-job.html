The Second Job

Leer este parte en español →

A crash is not over when the vehicles stop moving. In the worst wrecks the hardest minutes come after, while a door that will not open and a few feet of fuel line decide whether the people inside get out.

The pattern. Two vehicles meet head on. The closing speed is the sum of the two speeds, and the energy each one carries rises with the square of its speed (Closing Speed; Seven Times the Energy). A crash hard enough to fold the front of a vehicle can leave the people inside alive and still unable to get out on their own. This page names no one and decides nothing about any crash.

The fire. Fire is rare. A federal evaluation of the fuel-system safety standard reports: “In 1991 to 2001, 2.5 to 2.8 percent of passenger cars and LTVs involved in fatal crashes experienced post-crash fires,” LTVs being light trucks and vans. The same report calls them “infrequent but critically dangerous events” (NHTSA, Evaluation of FMVSS No. 301, Fuel System Integrity). The energy of a crash can rupture a fuel line or a tank, and a fuel-fed fire builds fast and burns hot, in the seconds a stunned or trapped person does not have (Fire).

What the law asks first. The first question is the ordinary one: who put two vehicles in the same lane. Texas requires a driver on a roadway of sufficient width to “drive on the right half of the roadway,” with exceptions such as passing (Transportation Code Section 545.051); requires drivers meeting each other to “move to or remain to the right” (Section 545.052); and forbids a speed “greater than is reasonable and prudent under the circumstances then existing” (Section 545.351). The fault for the collision is divided by percentages among the people whose conduct caused it (Comparative Fault).

The second question. The first report skips it. A vehicle has two jobs: to help avoid the crash, and to let the people inside come through it. Whether the fuel system held, whether a door could be opened, and how long a person was trapped belong to the second job, and the second job can be its own claim against the maker, alongside any claim against whoever caused the crash (Crashworthiness). In a Texas design-defect claim, the person bringing it must prove “there was a safer alternative design” and that the defect “was a producing cause” of the injury or death, and a safer design is one that would have “prevented or significantly reduced the risk” of the harm “without substantially impairing the product’s utility” and was “economically and technologically feasible” when the vehicle left the manufacturer’s or seller’s control (Civil Practice and Remedies Code Section 82.005).

The record. The wreck itself is the proof, and it does not last. A burned vehicle is towed, stored and sold for salvage, and the fuel line, the latch and the seat go with it, along with whatever data the vehicle kept. Ask for them to be preserved before they are hauled off (Spoliation).

Crashworthiness said a roof, a window or a door can decide who walks away from a crash none of them caused. Fire said that sometimes the crash is survivable and the fire is not. The Roof and the Tank asked whether a car was built to come through what happened to it. The minutes after the stop are where those questions are answered.

Sources. The pattern, without a news link: head-on crashes on Texas roads that leave people trapped, some followed by fire.
